# AI Use Declaration

**Project:** Redstep — Marswalk Planner  
**Challenge:** NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 13 November 2026

## Our disclosure

We used generative AI tools as development assistants while building Redstep. AI support was used for parts of the software-development and communication workflow; it was not a substitute for the team's project decisions, source research, testing, or responsibility for the final submission.

We are making this disclosure so that judges, reviewers, and users can understand where AI contributed and where human review was required.

## How AI was used

AI tools were used in a supporting capacity for tasks such as:

- **Programming assistance:** exploring implementation approaches, drafting or revising code, and helping explain unfamiliar technical concepts.
- **Debugging and optimization:** investigating errors, reviewing possible fixes, and suggesting performance improvements.
- **Documentation and writing:** helping structure and refine technical documentation, explanatory text, and project descriptions.
- **Planning and ideation:** brainstorming ways to present the project and communicate its features.

AI-generated suggestions were treated as proposals, not automatically correct answers. The team selected, edited, integrated, or rejected suggestions as appropriate.

## Human responsibility and verification

The team remains responsible for the design, implementation, integration, and final presentation of Redstep. In particular:

- The team chose the project's goals, features, user experience, and technical direction.
- Code suggested or revised with AI assistance was reviewed and tested as part of the development process. AI assistance does not mean every suggestion was accepted unchanged.
- Scientific claims, mission references, and source data should be checked against the primary sources listed in the application and its [Sources page](https://redstep.vercel.app/sources).
- Route-planning outputs are produced by the project's implemented algorithms, datasets, and configured assumptions. They should not be treated as verified real-world mission instructions.
- The team is accountable for errors, limitations, and decisions in the submitted project, regardless of whether AI assistance was involved.

## AI in the running application

Redstep's route assessments and guidance are generated by its implemented rules, calculations, and data-processing logic. The application does **not** rely on a generative-AI model to decide whether a route is GO, CAUTION, or NO-GO, or to produce its route-planning verdicts.

The presence of AI assistance during development should not be confused with an AI-powered feature in the live application.

## Data, imagery, and scientific integrity

AI tools were not used as a source of scientific measurements or as a replacement for mission datasets. Planetary maps, terrain products, rover information, and other scientific inputs are attributed through the project's source documentation.

AI-generated explanations or coding suggestions are not scientific evidence. Where a statement concerns Mars, a mission, a measurement, or a safety-related assumption, the appropriate reference is the cited dataset, publication, or mission source—not the AI tool that may have helped draft the wording.

Any AI-generated visual or audio material used in a future version or in promotional media should be identified separately and must not be presented as authentic NASA, JPL, USGS, ESA, or rover imagery.

## Limitations

AI tools can produce incorrect code, outdated information, misleading explanations, or plausible-sounding but unsupported claims. Human review reduces these risks but does not guarantee that the project is error-free. Redstep is an educational and research prototype, not an operational mission-planning system.

## Summary

**AI assisted parts of our development and documentation workflow. The team directed the project, reviewed and integrated the work, is responsible for the final result, and relies on attributed scientific sources—not AI-generated claims—for its Mars data.**
